# Brand

Defines how BuildX sounds — voice, personality, messaging, and the standing CTA policy.
Everything visual lives in `design-system.md`.

## What BuildX is

A Massachusetts ADU builder — the South Shore and surrounding towns. BuildX sells
**finished, all-in accessory dwelling units**: septic, survey, tree clearing, foundation,
build, final clean and landscaping prep included, with a published price range rather than
a quote-on-request.

Two things shape every piece of content:

- **The home tour is the core marketing motion.** Home tours are a core part of the
  marketing strategy, and the standing call to action is to come stand inside a finished
  ADU. The product is physical and the pitch is "come see it." Tour scheduling and cadence
  are not recorded here — any frequency claim must come from `verified-facts.md`.
- **The category is new and the rules are still moving.** Massachusetts ADU law, local
  bylaws and town procedure are actively changing, so a large share of the content is
  simply *accurate information*, delivered before a competitor gets it wrong.

Product models are named — the **X-9** appears throughout the tour footage. Numbers, prices
and counts are governed by `verified-facts.md`; nothing in this file should be treated as a
source for a figure.

## Voice

These are enduring editorial principles rather than marketing slogans. Future content should
follow the principles even when the exact examples change.

Six principles. Each is evidenced by verbatim transcript lines, quoted here as tone
reference — not as approved on-screen copy.

### 1. Plain and specific

Numbers, materials and timelines instead of adjectives. Where a competitor says "affordable
and beautiful," BuildX says the price, the square footage and the month count.

> *"It takes, from the day we start, it takes about four months to build. And then figure
> about four months for permits."*

### 2. Honest before flattering

The brand's central claim is accuracy. It is willing to contradict the market, name a
number the audience has seen advertised and call it false, and correct other builders
directly.

> *"a lot of people will start working with other contractors and they tell them all kinds
> of wrong information. Not even misleading, like dead wrong… what I'm telling you is
> truth."*

### 3. Teaching, not selling

Content leads with useful information the viewer can act on, and lets the qualification
happen by itself. This is stated explicitly on camera:

> *"It's an odd sales pitch. I'm gonna give you real information you can use."*
> *"You'll leave there with real answers."*

### 4. Zero pressure

Long decision cycles are treated as normal and healthy, never as an objection to overcome.
No urgency gimmicks, no manufactured scarcity, no discount deadlines.

> *"I had a customer sign last week that I met a year and a half ago… it's a big decision."*

### 5. Hedges honestly

Where something is believed but unproven, the uncertainty stays in. **This is a brand asset,
not a weakness** — the willingness to say "I haven't proven this yet" is what makes the
confident claims credible.

> *"I believe — and I still, I haven't proven this yet — that you can… sell it separately."*

The same instinct produces the "made up number" disclaimer when illustrating a payment
schedule. Both cases are load-bearing: see `verified-facts.md`, which records where a hedge
must survive into the caption.

### 6. Dry, self-deprecating humour

Never jokes at the customer's expense; often at its own.

> *"We take Bitcoin, Visa, Mastercard."*
> *"we do everything except tuck tuck you into bed"*
> *"I did my LiDAR… My software shows me the grade. I have all the toys."*

## Personality

**Warm, direct, unpolished, and deferential about the customer's own house.**

The delivery is conversational and imperfect — stutters, restarts and repeated words are
part of it. `editorial.md` governs when to keep them, but the brand position is that natural
delivery reads as honest and should not be smoothed into presenter-speak.

Authority is expressed through specifics and experience, never through status claims:

> *"this is your custom house… unless you're gonna do something stupid that's gonna hurt
> you, I'm gonna go along with what you wanna build."*
> *"Yeah, it's your house. We're gonna build the way you want it."*

The founding motivation is stated plainly and without polish, and is worth protecting as a
brand note:

> *"I fell in love with it in November of 24… just hearing everyone's stories and what they
> were trying to do."*

## Communication style

**Do**

- Address the viewer directly — second person, present tense.
- Lead with the specific: a number, a material, a rule, a timeline.
- State the limit or the exception rather than hiding it.
- Let a strong line stand alone without a qualifier chasing it.
- Keep the natural speech pattern in captions, including stutters, when it reads as human.

**Do not**

- Use superlatives or hype adjectives ("stunning," "luxury," "revolutionary").
- Manufacture urgency or scarcity.
- Paraphrase a spoken line into something tidier — **exact spoken dialogue only, never
  invent a line.** This is an absolute rule; see `editorial.md` and `captions.md`.
- Soften an honest hedge into a confident claim.
- Put a number on screen that has not been checked against `verified-facts.md`.
- Name a person or a town that was not named on camera. See `people.md`.

## Messaging pillars

The recurring arguments across the content. Each exists to overturn a specific belief the
audience already holds.

| Pillar | The belief it overturns |
|---|---|
| **Honest pricing** — the real all-in number, published | That advertised budget ADU prices are achievable |
| **Come see one** — the tour beats any amount of research online | That photos, floor plans and Google are enough |
| **Build for the next owner** — position and size it for what comes later | That an ADU is a one-time, standalone decision |
| **Don't build smaller to save** — fixed costs dominate | That square footage and price scale together |
| **Most of what you've been told is wrong** — including by other builders | That any contractor can answer ADU questions |
| **It's your house** — everything customises from a standard starting point | That a standard model means no choices |
| **Full scope, no surprises** — what is and is not included, stated plainly | That the quoted number will grow later |

Numbers supporting any of these are in `verified-facts.md`. Do not restate a figure here.

## Podcast branding

**Just Build Baby** — the BuildX podcast. Host and guest labelling conventions are in
`people.md`.

The title is the brand's tagline, and the two are deliberately the same phrase. Capitalise
by context:

- **Just Build Baby** — the podcast, as a title.
- **"Just build baby"** — the spoken signoff and the tagline in graphics copy.

### The signoff

> *"So there you have it. Remember, just build baby."*

This is the established outro. It appears naturally at the end of piece-to-camera clips and
functions as the **end beat before the CTA** — the line that closes the thought, after which
the standard end card runs.

Usage rules:

- **Never write it into a clip that does not already contain it.** It is spoken content,
  governed by the exact-dialogue rule.
- Where a clip ends on the signoff, cut straight from it into the end card — do not leave a
  gap, and do not add another closing line.
- It also appears as the gold kicker in graphics copy — *"BuildX. Just build baby."* See
  `design-system.md`.

## CTA policy

**The rule, in one sentence:**

> **Never author, invent or propose a call to action. Always append the standard BuildX end
> card.**

Both halves matter, and together they resolve what looked like a contradiction between two
sources:

| Source | What it says | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Session memory (`feedback_buildx_no_cta`) | *"Do not suggest, write, or include a CTA"* | Do not **write a new one** — a standard one already exists, so drafting CTA copy is wasted work |
| `SHORTS-PLAN.md` (clip 19 note, open flags) | *"same CTA on everything — no exceptions"* | Do not **omit it** — every short carries the standard end card, including emotionally weighted ones |

They are consistent. The first governs authorship; the second governs application.

### Applying it

- **Every short ends with the standard end card.** There are no exceptions, including for
  emotional or story-led clips. An earlier per-clip exception was proposed and explicitly
  overruled.
- Where the speaker delivers a spoken CTA on camera, that is content, not a replacement —
  it can precede the end card, but does not substitute for it.
- The standard end card should be treated as an existing reusable asset, not something to
  rebuild per project: see `assets.md` for its identity and scaling, and `design-system.md`
  for how the logo interacts with it.
- In clip analyses, hook outputs and strategy documents, **skip the "CTA" section
  entirely.** It is always the same and restating it wastes space.
